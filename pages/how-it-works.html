How usertypo_ scoring works

Last updated: September 3, 2026

1. Overview

Every completed typing test on usertypo_ ends with a small set of headline numbers: WPM, raw WPM, accuracy, and consistency. Those values are computed in the browser from what you typed, then—when you are signed in—saved to your session history so Profile stats, XP, and leaderboards can reuse them.

Under the hood, usertypo_ treats typing the way most modern typing sites do: one “word” is defined as five characters (letters, spaces, and punctuation all count). Speed is therefore characters-per-minute divided by five. Accuracy is keystroke-based, not “percentage of words that look correct.” Consistency measures how steady your second-by-second raw speed was, using a coefficient-of-variation transform inspired by the well-known Monkeytype “kogasa” mapping.

Guest tests still show live results and graphs. Signed-in tests also persist to Supabase Postgres, award XP, update personal bests, and may update leaderboards through a Cloudflare Worker.

2. Words Per Minute (WPM)

Net WPM (the big number on your results) is intended to reward clean typing. Only characters that count as correct contribute. On usertypo_, a finished word only contributes to net WPM if that word has no error styling left on it—no mistyped letters, no extra characters, no lingering error underlines. When a word qualifies, every non-extra character in it is counted, plus one character for the trailing space that separates words. The word you are currently typing contributes only the letters that are already marked correct (no trailing space yet).

Elapsed time is measured from the first keystroke that starts the test until the test ends. To avoid wild early spikes, the denominator never drops below two seconds (about 0.033 minutes) when computing final WPM.

WPM = ( validChars ÷ 5 ) elapsedMinutes
where
elapsedMinutes = max( elapsedMs ÷ 60000 , 2 ÷ 60 )
validChars = correct chars from clean finished words (+ spaces) + correct chars on the current word

The UI usually rounds net WPM for display. When a session is saved for a signed-in account, the database stores a higher-precision value (two decimal places) so personal bests and leaderboards can break ties fairly.

Optional characters-per-second (CPS) is a unit conversion of the same net speed:

CPS = WPM × 5 ÷ 60

3. Raw WPM

Raw WPM answers a different question: how fast were your fingers moving, regardless of whether the text ended up correct? Raw character counts include every character you actually typed on completed words (plus spaces), and every typed character on the current word— including mistakes and extras—while ignoring untouched slate letters that were never reached.

rawWPM = ( rawChars ÷ 5 ) elapsedMinutes

Comparing net and raw WPM is one of the fastest ways to diagnose your session. A large gap usually means you were correcting a lot, leaving errors unfixed, or both. A tiny gap with high accuracy means you typed cleanly at a stable pace.

Signal What it usually means
Raw ≈ net, high accuracy Clean typing with few corrections.
Raw ≫ net, high accuracy You fixed mistakes as you went (backspaces / retypes).
Raw ≫ net, lower accuracy Uncorrected errors and/or extras left in the text.
Both low Overall pace was slow—or the test ended early / failed a threshold.

4. Accuracy

Accuracy on usertypo_ is keystroke-based. Every keystroke you make during the test increments a total. Every incorrect keystroke increments an error counter. At the end:

accuracy% = max( 0 , totalKeystrokes − errorsMade totalKeystrokes × 100 )

If somehow no keystrokes were recorded, accuracy is treated as 0 for the final save path. Live UI may round for display; saved sessions keep finer precision.

This design matters because fixing a typo costs keystrokes too. Backspacing and retyping the correct letter can lower accuracy even when the final on-screen word looks perfect. That is intentional: accuracy reflects control over the keyboard stream, not only the final rendered string.

5. Consistency

Consistency asks whether your speed was smooth or bursty. On the solo home test, usertypo_ samples raw WPM once per second while you type. That series becomes the input to a coefficient-of-variation calculation, then a nonlinear map (often called a kogasa transform) that compresses the result into a friendly 0–100 score.

If fewer than 2 samples, consistency = 100
otherwise
mean = average( rawWpmPerSecond )
stdDev = √( Σ ( x − mean )² ÷ n )
cov = stdDev mean
kogasa = 100 × ( 1 − tanh( cov + cov³÷3 + cov⁵÷5 ) )
consistency = clamp( round( kogasa ) , 0 , 100 )

High consistency means your per-second raw speeds clustered tightly around the mean. Low consistency usually means surges and stalls—common when hunting for personal bests with uneven rhythm, or when a hard word forces a pause. If mean ≤ 0, consistency stays at 100.

Multiplayer duel/room races can compute consistency from inter-keystroke intervals instead of the per-second raw series. The kogasa mapping is the same; the input signal differs. Always compare consistency within the same mode family.

6. Home result graphs

After a solo test, the home page can open a stats view with a basic or advanced graph. Both share the same timeline: the X-axis is seconds from test start, and the Y-axis is WPM with a “nice” maximum snapped upward in steps of 30. Failed threshold tests clear history series and skip drawing—there is no graph for a run that did not qualify as a completed measurement.

6.1 Basic graph

The basic graph focuses on three readable signals:

  • Solid primary line — cumulative average net WPM over time (how your overall pace evolved).
  • Dashed gray line — instantaneous raw WPM for that second.
  • Red error dots — non-hesitation error markers placed at the second they occurred.
rawWPMsecond = ( keystrokesThisSecond ÷ 5 ) × 60

A soft fill under the net WPM line uses average word difficulty (based on word length bands) to tint how “heavy” the passage felt. Hovering interpolates WPM/raw and shows how many errors landed on that second. Optional settings can force the Y-axis to start at zero and can smooth the polyline with a Catmull-Rom-style curve.

6.2 Advanced graph

The advanced graph keeps the same net/raw lines, then layers richer diagnostics for people who want to study how a score was made.

  • Cadence ribbon — around the raw line, a band whose half-width grows with the coefficient of variation of inter-key intervals inside that second. Wide ribbons mean uneven key timing.
  • Word difficulty columns — vertical clips of the WPM fill sized by how long each word took on the timeline, so hard or slow words visually thicken the chart.
  • Typed error DNA — shaped markers for mistype, reversal, overshoot, missed, extra, and hesitation (hesitations use a distinct amber square).
  • Zoom — Ctrl + mouse wheel changes how many words are visible; you can pan horizontally through the passage.
ribbonHalfWidth = min( 28 , cv × rawWPM × 0.55 )

If fewer than two inter-key intervals exist in that second, a small default band (±3) is used instead.

7. Userstats page cards

The Profile / Stats page /userstats is a history dashboard. Almost everything there is derived from your saved typing_sessions rows, plus compact diagnostics attached to recent tests, progression state, and optional leaderboard rank lookups.

7.1 Profile header & progression ring

Username, avatar, and visibility settings come from your profile. The level ring, title, XP into level, streak, and percent-to-next come from the progression API (see Section 8). The ring’s arc is a visual of percentToNext.

7.2 Timed bests & word bests

Standard presets get dedicated personal-best cards: timed 15 / 30 / 60 / 120 seconds and words 10 / 25 / 50 / 100. For each mode+amount, usertypo_ scans non-failed sessions and keeps the row with the highest WPM (accuracy is taken from that same best row). Custom amounts you invent in the footer picker do not get a dedicated best card here—even though those sessions still appear in history and totals.

7.3 Summary totals

Tests counted, total time typed, and words typed summarize your whole history— not just standard presets:

totalSeconds = Σ durationSeconds
totalWords = Σ round( correctChars ÷ 5 )

7.4 Performance over time

A Chart.js timeline plots filtered sessions as Speed (WPM), Accuracy, rolling averages (avg of 10 / avg of 100 newest-first), personal-best step markers, and a least-squares estimate of speed change per hour. Filters let you focus on a mode family so the trend is not polluted by mixing 10-word sprints with 120s endurance runs.

7.5 Error diagnostics & hardware hotspots

For recent tests, usertypo_ stores a compact diagnostics summary (capped roughly to your last ~50 diagnostic rows). Aggregates power:

  • Error anatomy donut (mistype / reversal / overshoot / missed / extra shares)
  • Top troublesome bigrams and words
  • Key confusion pairs (expected vs typed)
  • Hardware hotspot heatmap on a keymap, plus top characters by error count
  • Hand biometrics estimates from finger timing when available

7.6 Activity tracker & score distribution

The activity heatmap buckets completed (non-failed) tests by UTC day—similar in spirit to contribution calendars. Score distribution bins every completed WPM into 5-WPM ranges from 0 upward:

upperBound = ceil( maxWpm ÷ 5 ) × 5

Each bin then reports its count; the card also shows average, min, and max WPM across the filtered completed tests.

7.7 Test history table

A paginated table lists individual sessions with mode, WPM, raw, accuracy, consistency, errors, duration, language flags, and whether the row was a personal best or a failed threshold attempt. Failed rows remain visible for honesty, but they do not update bests, XP, or leaderboards.

8. XP, levels & titles

Experience points are awarded server-side when a non-failed session with positive WPM is inserted. The client may show an estimate for guests, but the database function is the source of truth for signed-in accounts.

8.1 Level curve

xpNeeded(L) = floor( 100 × L1.45 )
percentToNext = round( ( xpIntoLevel ÷ xpToNext ) × 1000 ) ÷ 10

Titles currently map roughly as: Novice (<10), Typist (≥10), Fluent (≥25), Sharp (≥50), Elite (≥75), Legend (≥100). The title is cosmetic; the level number and XP math are what progression uses. Level needs are at least 1 XP.

8.2 XP award formula

Failed or zero-WPM sessions award nothing. Otherwise:

base = min( 80 , max( 1 , floor( 1200 ÷ durationSeconds ) ) )
accMult = clamp( accuracy ÷ 100 , 0 , 1 )
modMult = 1.15 if punctuation or numbers is on, else 1.0
streakMult = 2.0 (≥14d) / 1.3 (≥7d) / 1.15 (≥3d) / 1.0
pbBonus = 50 if personal best, else 0
then
rawXp = max( 1 , floor( base × accMult × wpmMult × modMult × streakMult ) ) + pbBonus
awarded = softCap( dailyXpSoFar , rawXp )

wpmMult starts simple when you have few samples (1.25 if WPM ≥ 80, 1.1 if ≥ 50, else 1.0). Later it compares against your recent average (1.25 if ≥ 1.15× avg, 1.1 if ≥ 1.05×, else 1.0). Consistency is not used in the XP product. After 400 XP in a UTC day, further gains are soft-capped at ×0.25 (at least 1 XP).

9. Leaderboard rankings

Leaderboards cover the same standard presets as best cards (timed 15/30/60/120 and words 10/25/50/100), across daily, weekly, and all-time windows. Scores are ingested by a Cloudflare Worker that reads the saved Postgres session—never by writing rankings directly from the browser.

  • Opt out anytime with Show on leaderboard in settings.
  • Hard safety cap: WPM above 500 is rejected.
  • Daily / weekly boards accept any valid non-failed score for a supported preset.
  • All-time boards require ≥ 50 completed tests overall and a qualifying score of WPM ≥ 30. Until you reach 50 tests, you will not appear on all-time rankings (and existing all-time entries are cleared for that account).
  • Rank ordering prefers higher WPM, then higher accuracy, then earlier timestamp for ties.
Personal bests, XP, and leaderboards are related but not identical. A PB updates your Profile cards immediately. Leaderboard eligibility can still block an otherwise strong score if privacy, WPM caps, or all-time sample-size rules apply.

10. Glossary

Term Meaning on usertypo_
Word (for WPM) Five characters of typed text, including spaces and punctuation.
Net WPM Speed from valid/correct characters only.
Raw WPM Speed from all typed characters, including mistakes.
Accuracy Share of keystrokes that were correct.
Consistency 0–100 smoothness score from speed variance (kogasa map).
Personal best (PB) Highest non-failed WPM for a mode+amount; flagged in the database.
Standard preset Timed 15/30/60/120 or words 10/25/50/100.
Failed test Run that hit a fail threshold; saved for history but excluded from bests/XP/boards.
